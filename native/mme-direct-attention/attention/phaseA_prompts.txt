A quiet harbor town wakes up on a cool autumn morning, and the camera begins high above the water, drifting slowly down toward the docks as the first light breaks over the hills behind the town. Thin mist hangs over the bay, and the surface of the water is almost perfectly still except for the long silver wakes of two small fishing boats heading out past the lighthouse at the end of the stone breakwater. The lighthouse is white with a red band near the top, and its lamp is still turning, sweeping a pale beam across the mist even though the sky is already bright. Along the waterfront, a row of narrow houses painted in faded blue, yellow, green and rose leans gently toward the harbor, their windows catching the low sun one after another as the camera passes. Gulls circle above the masts of the moored sailboats, calling to each other, and one lands on a wooden post covered in green weed and barnacles. On the dock, an old fisherman in a heavy wool sweater and a knitted cap coils a thick rope into a neat spiral, pausing to rub his cold hands together and look out at the water. Beside him a younger woman in a yellow rain jacket stacks blue plastic crates of ice, and the crates knock together with a hollow sound. A black and white dog trots along the planks, sniffing at the crates, then sits down next to the old man and watches the boats. The camera continues at a slow walking pace along the quay, past a small cafe where a waiter wipes down round metal tables and sets out chairs, and the smell of fresh bread seems to drift through the frame as steam rises from a vent above the door. A bicycle with a wicker basket full of flowers leans against a lamppost. Further along, a market stall is being set up under a striped awning, and a woman arranges wet, shining fish on a bed of crushed ice, holding up a silver mackerel to the light. The sun climbs higher and the mist begins to thin, revealing green hills dotted with sheep and a narrow road winding up toward a small white chapel with a bell tower. The chapel bell begins to ring, slow and clear, and the sound carries across the water. The fishing boats are now small shapes near the horizon, their engines a faint, steady hum. A child in a red coat runs down a set of stone steps toward the water, holding a paper boat, and crouches at the edge of a slipway to set it on the surface, where it bobs and slowly turns in the gentle current. The camera lowers to water level and follows the little paper boat as it drifts out between the hulls of the moored boats, their reflections rippling in green and blue. Natural ambient sound only: gulls, the bell, rope and wood creaking, water lapping against the hulls, distant engines, footsteps on the planks and soft voices. No music, no voiceover.
At golden hour, a red fox trots along a frosty ridge above a misty pine valley. The camera follows from a low angle as the fox pauses, ears turning toward the distant call of a raven, then leaps over a fallen log dusted with snow. Its breath forms small clouds in the cold air, and dry grass crunches under its paws. Far below, a narrow river glints between the trees while a light wind moves through the branches. The fox reaches a rocky outcrop, sits, and looks out over the valley as the sun touches the mountains. Natural ambient sound only: wind, footsteps on frozen ground, a raven calling, the faint rush of the river. No music, no voiceover.
